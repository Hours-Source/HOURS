What an hour of each occupation is minted at, and why

Find an occupation to see its reference multiplier and every step that produced it: the measured inputs, the weights that were chosen, and the frozen scale that turns a score into a multiplier. Add a second occupation to see exactly where the two differ.

The reference multiplier sets the floor at which one hour of this work is minted as TEH. It is not a wage and says nothing about what the work earns above that floor.

      Each tick is one occupation, placed on the multiplier scale (log spacing) Band the employment-weighted average is required to sit in — a convention: R was solved so the average lands in it
      What if the factor weights were different?

      The four factor weights are and uncalibrated. The methodology tests each within ±0.10. Move them here to see how much a result depends on that choice. Weights are rescaled to sum to 1. The scale stays frozen , exactly as the repo's sensitivity harness holds it: a score that moves past either frozen end is clipped at ×1.0 or at the top of the scale, and the number clipped is shown. These are explorations, not published values.

      Read the order and the ratio first — they are what the data measures. The average and the band are shown for completeness; they are conventions.
